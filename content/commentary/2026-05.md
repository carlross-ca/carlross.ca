_This is a personal investing journal, not investment advice or an offer to manage money._

## What happened

May is the first month I am treating as part of the public journal. The goal is simple: document the portfolio clearly enough that the process, mistakes, risk, and results can be followed over time.

Markets were cooperative enough to let the reporting process matter more than the drama. The month was mainly about getting the daily data pull, reconciliation, journaling, and public note format into a repeatable shape.

## What I did

I used ETFs as the portfolio base and options selectively where the risk/reward looked worth tracking. Sizing was deliberately modest because this first month was more about proving the workflow than forcing activity.

No single trade or theme is supposed to define the journal. The main work is keeping the process honest: write down the reason for a trade, size it before the outcome is known, and review it without pretending hindsight was foresight.

## What I learned

The first useful lesson is that the monthly note is only as good as the daily record underneath it. If the trade rationale is written when the trade is placed, the monthly review is straightforward. If the rationale has to be reconstructed later, the review gets slippery.

The second lesson is that a public format changes behavior in a good way. It makes vague thinking more visible.

## What I am watching next

For June, I want to keep the same focus: simple notes, clean data, disciplined sizing, and fewer explanations that only make sense after the outcome is known.
